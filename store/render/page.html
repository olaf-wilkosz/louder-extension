The quiet comeback of reading aloud

By Mara Keller · 6 min read

Long before printing presses, reading was something people did together, out loud. A page was a small performance, and the listener's ear caught what a hurried eye would skip. Today the habit is returning quietly, one browser tab at a time.

Listening to an article while you cook, stretch or rest your eyes changes how it lands. Sentences you would have skimmed get their full length, and the shape of an argument is easier to follow when every clause arrives in order.

Writers notice it first. A paragraph that looked fine on screen sounds clumsy at normal speed, and a stray comma suddenly demands a breath. Hearing your own draft read back is the cheapest editor there is.

There is a practical side too. Long reads pile up in a browser, and not every evening leaves room for them. Turning a saved page into something you can listen to on a walk gives those tabs a second life.
